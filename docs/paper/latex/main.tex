\title{Where Do Long-Term Memory Agents Lose Evidence?\\[2pt]
\large An audited attribution protocol, a corrected oracle ladder, and
architecture-conditional failure localisation}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
When a long-term memory agent answers incorrectly, accuracy alone cannot say
where the failure happened. We present an evidence-centric attribution
protocol that follows each question's gold evidence through the lifecycle
(formation, storage, retrieval, context, reasoning) and labels wrong answers
with the first observable evidence-loss boundary (F1--F5), plus labels for
evidence-insufficient (E0) and instrument-uncertain (EVAL) cases. Because such claims are only as good as
their instruments, we audit ours against 200 human-labelled questions:
failure labels are 90\% human-confirmed, but the judge accepts
abstention-style answers (30.3\% of sampled stored-correct rows are
human-wrong), and the automatic sufficiency gate is near-independent of
humans ($\kappa$ 0.03--0.07) until rebuilt as a per-fact task
($\kappa$ 0.835). The human stage instrument is reliable ($\kappa$ 0.897);
the automatic first-loss label is not ($\kappa$ 0.59--0.71) and never emits
E0 or rerank. With corrected instruments we find (i) the memory-induced loss
is benchmark-dependent---the corrected real $\to$ oracle-memory $\to$
oracle-context ladder is 0.306 $\to$ 0.682 $\to$ 0.761 on LoCoMo (all rungs
significant) but 0.479 $\to$ 0.676 $\to$ 0.799 on LongMemEval (last rung
not); and (ii) on LoCoMo $n{=}100$, three systems with indistinguishable
accuracy (0.407/0.423/0.429; McNemar $p = 0.736/0.728/0.864$) fail at
significantly different stages (Stuart--Maxwell, Holm
$p \leq 2.8\times10^{-4}$). Two pre-registered ablations move stages in the
predicted direction but below the pre-registered bar; we therefore report
architecture-conditional localisation, retract a universal-fingerprint claim,
and show that stage-targeted repair does not beat a no-op placebo.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Long-term memory is now standard in LLM agents: history is written to a store,
retrieved at answer time, and packed into context. Benchmarks such as LoCoMo
and LongMemEval \citep{maharana-etal-2024-locomo,wu2025longmemeval} measure
answers over long histories but report final-answer accuracy. When two systems
score the same, accuracy cannot distinguish \emph{where} each fails---a system
that never wrote the fact looks identical to one that wrote it and never
retrieved it. Repair and understanding require localising the failure.

This paper asks a measurement question: \textbf{can the earliest point at
which the evidence for an answer becomes unobservable be located reliably,
and what does that location mean?} Attribution is not a blank
slate---HaluMem \citep{chen2025halumem} grades operations, MemOps
\citep{hao2026memops} benchmarks lifecycle operations on synthetic traces, and
MemTrace \citep{deng2026memtrace} formalises the earliest decisive faulty
operation on an instrumented graph with 160 annotated failures---so we make
\textbf{no priority claim} (\S\ref{sec:related}). What the prior lines lack is
a \emph{human-calibrated instrument}, controlled oracle conditions, and a
pre-registered architecture test; those gaps are where our claims live.

We make four contributions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1 --- An evidence-centric attribution protocol
(\S\ref{sec:protocol}).} A frozen pipeline that follows each question's gold
evidence through the artifacts a system exposes and labels every wrong answer
with the \textbf{first observable evidence-loss boundary} (F1--F6 formation to
reasoning, E0 evidence-insufficient, EVAL instrument uncertainty). It is an
observability boundary, not a causal cause, and needs no source
instrumentation, at stage rather than operation granularity.

\item \textbf{C2 --- A human audit of the instruments (\S\ref{sec:audit}).}
90\% of failure labels are human-confirmed; abstention false positives
are 30.3\% of sampled stored-correct rows (13/13-precise guarded correction vs
52\% naive); the automatic sufficiency gate is
near-independent of humans ($\kappa$ 0.03--0.07) until rebuilt as per-fact
annotation ($\kappa$ 0.835/0.889); and the human stage instrument is reliable
($\kappa$ 0.897) while the automatic first-loss label is not
($\kappa$ 0.59--0.71). To our knowledge no prior memory-failure study reports
this calibration.

\item \textbf{C3 --- A corrected oracle ladder (\S\ref{sec:ladder}).} Real
memory vs oracle memory vs oracle context quantifies memory's cost---after
abstention correction. The ladder is 0.306 $\to$ 0.682 $\to$ 0.761 on LoCoMo
(all rungs significant) and 0.479 $\to$ 0.676 $\to$ 0.799 on LongMemEval (last
rung not): benchmark-dependent, with corrections moving rung conclusions in
both directions. No operation-level diagnostic includes such controls.

\item \textbf{C4 --- Architecture-conditional localisation
(\S\ref{sec:localisation}).} On LoCoMo $n{=}100$, three systems with
indistinguishable accuracy (McNemar $p = 0.736/0.728/0.864$) fail at
significantly different stages (Stuart--Maxwell, Holm $p \leq
2.8\times10^{-4}$). Such differences have been reported descriptively
(MemTrace, Fig.~4b); our addition is a pre-registered within-system ablation
(A1: F1 share 0.13 $\to$ 0.00, $p = 0.0031$, accuracy unchanged; A3: F4
0.37 $\to$ 0.23) that moves stages in the predicted direction but below the
20-point bar.
\end{itemize}

We state three negative results up front. \textbf{(N1)} The
\emph{universal}-failure-fingerprint framing is \textbf{retracted}: the
pre-registered decision gate returned partial, and the phenomenon is
architecture-conditional. \textbf{(N2)} Localisation is \textbf{not shown to
be actionable}: repairing the measured retrieval stage moved recall
0.043 $\to$ 0.321 but produced only +7 net answer flips (McNemar $p = 0.143$,
CI $[-0.029, +0.441]$), inside the noise floor of a no-op placebo (9/35
flips). \textbf{(N3)} The automatic first-loss label is \textbf{not a per-row
instrument} ($\kappa$ 0.59--0.71, and it never emits E0 or rerank); stage
claims are distributional, on 90\%-confirmed labels, and survive label noise
drawn from the measured confusion (\S\ref{sec:localisation}).

\S\ref{sec:related} reviews related work; \S\ref{sec:protocol} defines the
protocol; \S\ref{sec:audit} audits the instruments; \S\ref{sec:ladder} and
\S\ref{sec:localisation} present the results; the appendices give
reproducibility detail and a claim-to-artifact map.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Memory benchmarks}

LoCoMo \citep{maharana-etal-2024-locomo} established the standard setting:
long multi-session histories, questions afterwards, final-answer scoring.
LongMemEval \citep{wu2025longmemeval} evaluates five abilities---extraction,
multi-session and temporal reasoning, knowledge update, abstention---and
conceptualises memory as indexing, retrieval, and reading. MemBench
\citep{tan-etal-2025-membench} adds memory-type and cost dimensions;
MemoryAgentBench \citep{hu2025memoryagentbench} evaluates incremental
interaction across four competencies; RealMem \citep{bian2026realmem}
interleaves queries; LoCoMo-Plus \citep{li2026locomoplus} probes implicit
constraints.

These benchmarks differ in coverage, not in what a score means: the system is
judged on final answers. LongMemEval's stage vocabulary notwithstanding, none
reports, for a wrong answer, where the system's own pipeline first stopped
exposing the required evidence---the question this paper measures. An
attribution protocol is orthogonal to benchmark choice and consumes whatever
gold evidence is provided, which is why we run it on two benchmarks rather
than proposing a third.

\subsection{Operation-level and lifecycle diagnostics}

Three recent lines move inside the pipeline; we state each relationship
because one defines our problem.

\textbf{HaluMem} \citep{chen2025halumem} introduced operation-level
hallucination grading: gold memory points and update pairs are annotated, and
a system is scored separately on extraction, updating, and QA over synthetic
histories. It shows that extraction and update errors propagate to QA---a
pattern our labels also expose---but its gold states are benchmark-generated,
its scoring is a single uncalibrated judge (annotation validates the dataset,
not the instrument), and retrieval enters only through recall; it checks
operations, not where evidence disappeared.

\textbf{MemOps} \citep{hao2026memops} benchmarks whether systems can
\emph{detect, execute, and respect} lifecycle operations---remember, forget,
update, reflect, and compositions---embedded in synthetic conversations with
gold traces and six probe families. Its question is ``can the system carry out
the right operation?'', ours is ``for an already-wrong answer, which stage
first stopped exposing the evidence?'' The authors list judge calibration as
future work; we perform it.

\textbf{MemTrace} \citep{deng2026memtrace} is the closest work and the reason
this paper makes no priority claim. It locates the \emph{earliest decisive
faulty operation} in an execution graph obtained by instrumenting system
code; MemTraceBench provides 160 human-annotated failures from
LoCoMo/LongMemEval/RealMem over four systems, with operation-identification
accuracy from 14\% to 58\%, plus a prompt-optimisation application. Its
taxonomy distinguishes annotation errors (gold insufficient---our E0) and
LLM-judge errors, which we also treat as first-class. We differ along four
axes:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Object of tracing.} MemTrace follows operations and variables in
an instrumented graph; we follow a question's gold evidence through whatever
artifacts the system exposes. Ours needs no instrumentation and applies to
black-box systems, but it cannot name the faulty line of code and inherits the
benchmark's gold-evidence completeness.
\item \textbf{Instrument calibration.} MemTrace records but excludes
annotation and judge errors (67 and 3 of 230 cases) without quantifying the
judge's error rates. Our audit measures them---30.3\% abstention false
positives, gate--human $\kappa$ 0.03--0.07, a rebuilt per-fact gate at
$\kappa$ 0.835---and shows which conclusions the correction changes.
\item \textbf{Controlled conditions.} MemTrace compares systems as they are; we
add an oracle ladder that attributes loss to memory versus context versus
generation. None of the lifecycle-diagnostic lines above includes such
controls.
\item \textbf{Pre-registered ablation.} MemTrace reports cross-system error
differences (its Fig.~4b) descriptively. We test the same observation with a
pre-registered gate and within-system ablations, and report the gate as
partial rather than renegotiating the bar.
\end{enumerate}

Appendix Table~\ref{tab:methods} summarises the comparison; entries are from
the cited papers unless marked $\dagger$ (measured here). There is no
head-to-head run (Limitations).


\subsection{Memory systems}

We cite the major families as context, not evaluated systems: extraction-and-update stores \citep{chhikara2025mem0},
context-window operating systems \citep{packer2023memgpt}, agentic
note-organisation \citep{xu2025amem}, graph-augmented retrieval
\citep{gutierrez2024hipporag}, self-controlled and session-summary memory
\citep{wang2024scm,pan2025secom}, and unified memory layers
\citep{li2025memos}. Our evaluated systems (\S\ref{sec:localisation}) are
controlled implementations of the extraction, compression, and
raw-turn-retrieval families, each chosen for a known, removable bottleneck;
we do not claim they are the strongest of their families.

\subsection{Reliability of automatic evaluation}

LLM-as-judge studies document position and verbosity bias
\citep{zheng2023judging,wang2024notfairevaluators}, prompt-sensitivity
\citep{liu2023geval}, self-preference \citep{panickssery2024selfpreference},
and systematic bias families
\citep{ye2025justice,chen2024humansjudge,shi2025judgingjudges}; agreement has
its own methodology \citep{artstein2008kappa}. Our audit isolates a
task-specific failure mode: in a memory pipeline the judge \textbf{rewards
abstention}. Of rows stored as correct, 30.3\% are human-wrong, 13/20 being
abstentions accepted as answers; because abstention is first-class in
LongMemEval \citep{wu2025longmemeval}, a naive correction is only 52\%
precise while a reference-guarded rule is 13/13. The point is that here the
correction \emph{moves paired significance conclusions} (\S\ref{sec:ladder}),
not that judges are biased.

\subsection{Failure attribution in agent systems}

Agent-trajectory attribution localises faulty steps within a single task
run---via sampling and process signals, LLM trace inspection, or structured
graphs \citep{zhang2025whoandwhen,agentracer2025,bonagiri2026causalflow}; see
also the MAS failure taxonomy \citep{cemri2025whymasfail}. Memory failures
violate the core assumption: the faulty step may lie sessions earlier, and
the trace is not a short ordered log. MemTrace extends graph-based
attribution with instrumentation; we take the protocol route---observable
artifacts, no instrumentation---and pay in granularity. The routes are
complementary, and the audit of \S\ref{sec:audit} applies to either.

\section{The attribution protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The protocol observes only what a memory system exposes---what it writes, what
it retrieves, and what it assembles into the answer context---so it applies to
black-box deployments; it never reads a system's source code or requires
instrumentation. Its ground-truth signal is each benchmark's own gold evidence.

\subsection{Conditions}

Each question runs under up to three conditions: \textbf{Real Memory (RM)}, the
benchmark conversation ingested by the real system; \textbf{Oracle Memory
(OM)}, the gold evidence written directly into a fresh store and retrieved
through the real retrieval/rerank/context path (formation and storage
controlled and unmeasured); and \textbf{Oracle Context (OC)}, the gold
evidence placed directly in the answer context (all earlier stages
unmeasured).

The RM $\to$ OM $\to$ OC ladder isolates how much accuracy is recovered when
upstream stages are progressively perfectioned (\S\ref{sec:ladder});
Figure~\ref{fig:lifecycle} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) summarises the stages
and conditions.


\subsection{Lifecycle stages and evidence matching}

Each question carries gold evidence with provenance; the tracer measures its
recall at each stage (formation $\to$ storage $\to$ evolution $\to$ retrieval
$\to$ rerank $\to$ context; matching rules in
Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Evolution (F3) needs an atomic-fact store and
cannot fire on these runs.

\subsection{First observable evidence-loss boundary}

\emph{Terminology.} We report the \textbf{first observable evidence-loss
boundary}---the earliest stage at which the evidence required for the
reference answer stops being observable along the traced pipeline. We do \emph{not} call this a causal root cause: it says where evidence
became unobservable, not which mechanism counterfactually caused the wrong
answer. (Where ``first-loss'' appears in
code and artifacts it is shorthand for this boundary.)

\subsection{Answer evaluation and failure labels}

Model answers are judged semantically against the benchmark reference, with
deterministic floors around the judge (lexical support; repair restricted to
question-related candidates, never refusals; an \emph{ordering-refutation}
layer for two-option questions whose evidence resolves dates). Failure
attribution assigns the first applicable label in a fixed order
(Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). The
evidence-sufficiency gate (E0) is judged \textbf{against the benchmark
reference answer, never the model prediction}, so it is non-circular. F6 is a
\emph{candidate}: the evidence reached the context and the answer was still
wrong---consistent with a reasoning failure but also with evaluation error,
which \S\ref{sec:audit} quantifies.

\subsection{Label versions and reporting discipline}

Because raw labels carry instrument error (\S\ref{sec:audit}), every number
states its version: \textbf{auto} (stored judge/gate labels);
\textbf{abstention-corrected} (auto + the validated abstention rule; used for
accuracy); \textbf{human-gated} (corrected + the consensus gate on annotated
questions; used to re-attribute E0). Accuracy is \emph{semantic accuracy on
judged rows}, never the fraction of all rows correct. E0 is a relative pattern
across conditions, never a level, except where the human gate covers the
question.

\subsection{What the protocol does not establish}

The protocol states three boundaries: labels are observability boundaries,
not causal mechanisms; F6 is a candidate class; and per-row first-loss reads
are not a validated instrument (N3)---stage claims are distributional, on
labels whose direction the audit found 90\% sound.

\section{Auditing the instruments}
\label{sec:audit}

An attribution paper makes two kinds of claim: that an answer was wrong, and
that a particular lifecycle boundary was where the evidence first became
unobservable. Both rest on instruments---a semantic judge and an
evidence-sufficiency gate---and if those instruments are not audited, the
attribution map is a map of instrument error. This section reports the audit;
it absorbs and supersedes our earlier standalone instrument note.

\subsection{Audit design}

Two annotators labelled a stratified 200-question sample (100 LoCoMo $+$ 100
LongMemEval) blind to stored verdicts; disagreements were adjudicated. Fields:
answer correctness, evidence sufficiency. A separate 40-question package
(two annotators) measured \emph{stage}-annotation reliability
(\S\ref{sec:audit:stage}).

\subsection{Answer correctness is measurable; failure labels are mostly sound}

Answer-correctness agreement is high ($\kappa = 0.92$, $n = 177$; 7 paraphrase
disagreements adjudicated correct). Against adjudicated labels the judge
reaches P 0.697 / R 0.885 / F1 0.780 ($\kappa = 0.591$), with a benchmark gap
(LoCoMo F1 0.605; LongMemEval 0.880). The base rate survives review:
\textbf{54 of 60 stored-incorrect rows (90.0\%) are confirmed wrong}.

\subsection{The judge rewards abstention; a guarded rule corrects it}

Of 66 sampled rows the judge stored as semantically correct, \textbf{20
(30.3\%) are human-wrong}, 13 of them abstention-style answers the judge
accepted; uncorrected, accuracy is inflated wherever a model declines to answer---oracle
conditions most. A naive ``abstention is wrong''
rule is only \textbf{52\% precise} on the 25 sampled abstentions (12 are
LongMemEval \texttt{\_abs} references); a reference-guarded rule flips only
when the reference is a concrete proposition and identifies \textbf{all 13
human-wrong abstentions while preserving all 12 human-correct ones} (precision
and recall 1.00 on the subset). On the 77-row sample the rule flips 13 of 31
human-wrong rows (recall 0.42).

\subsection{The evidence gate needed three rounds to become an instrument}

The round-1 sufficiency field was degenerate ($\kappa = 0.0$: one annotator
never said ``no'') and a round-2 derivability checklist still failed
($\kappa = 0.181$, $n = 177$; the failure mode is documented in
Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Round 3 decomposed each reference into atomic
propositions (276 facts), had annotators mark one binary fact-presence
judgement per row, and aggregated transparently. This cleared the
pre-registered bar ($\kappa \geq 0.6$ on both levels):

Against the 200 adjudicated labels (178 consensus rows), the consensus gate
reaches precision 0.837 / recall 1.000 / F1 0.911, versus the stored gate's
0.244 / 0.528 / 0.333 on the same rows. Because the fact decomposition was
authored with access to the adjudicated labels, we treat the inter-annotator
$\kappa$ as the independent validation and the adjudication comparison as
corroboration.

The stored gate is near-independent of humans ($\kappa$ 0.03--0.07): it
\textbf{falsely marks 65 sufficient rows insufficient} (28 LoCoMo, 37
LongMemEval, mostly \texttt{\_abs}) and \textbf{misses 17} truly-insufficient
rows, so its E0 counts are biased upward and used only for relative comparison
(one shared gate per question). On annotated questions we re-attribute with
the consensus gate (LoCoMo 56/40/30 $\to$ 51/30/25; LongMemEval 13/13/15 $\to$
5/8/8), converting removed rows to F1/F4/F6/RC.

\subsection{The human stage instrument is reliable; the automatic first-loss label is not}
\label{sec:audit:stage}

A separate two-annotator package asked humans to assign the first
evidence-loss stage on 40 LoCoMo questions from the $n{=}100$ fingerprint run,
with full retrieval/context previews and an explicit ``preview absence = stage
missing'' rule. After a failed v1 ($\kappa = 0.685$ on valid rows; 13/40 rows
used an unmeasured label), the v2 instrument passed its pre-registered bar:
agreement 0.925, \textbf{multiclass $\kappa = 0.897$} (bar 0.70), no uncertain
rows.

Against the human labels, the automatic first-loss label reaches only
$\kappa = 0.705$ (annotator a) and $\kappa = 0.590$ (annotator b). The
disagreements concentrate on E0 (humans call 4 rows E0 where the automatic
trace assigns formation/retrieval) and on rerank (humans see 3 rerank losses
the automatic label calls retrieval). \textbf{The human stage instrument is
reliable; the automatic first-loss label is not a per-row instrument.} All
stage-distribution claims in \S\ref{sec:localisation} are therefore made at
the distribution level on failure labels (90\% human-confirmed,
\S\ref{sec:audit}), not on per-row first-loss reads.

\textbf{Where exactly the automatic label errs.} Per-class quantities on the
consensus rows ($n = 37$ where both annotators agree; consensus
$\kappa = 0.680$, agreement 0.784---a filtered subset, not directly
comparable to the per-annotator $\kappa$ above); per-class numbers are in
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:perclass}.


The instrument is dependable on the classes it emits (formation F1 0.88,
none 1.00, retrieval 0.81---recall 1.00, precision 0.68 because it absorbs
misplaced rows) and its errors are structural: it \textbf{never emits E0 or
rerank}. Per row it is a usable formation/retrieval/none classifier, unable to
separate E0 or rerank; as a distribution it carries the signal
\S\ref{sec:localisation} uses, stress-tested against this confusion.

\subsection{Reporting rules we adopt and recommend}

We follow three rules: (i) deterministic floors before trusting a judge;
(ii) report human agreement for every judge-derived dimension and mark sub-bar
dimensions non-citable (automatic E0 levels, per-row first-loss); (iii) report
auto and corrected numbers together, the delta being measurement error.

\section{How much of the loss is memory? The corrected oracle ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

The RM $\to$ OM $\to$ OC ladder (\S\ref{sec:protocol}) quantifies how much
accuracy is recovered when formation/storage (OM) and then everything upstream
of the answer (OC) are perfectioned. Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} and Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:ladder} report semantic accuracy on judged rows, $n = 200$ per
benchmark, on both label versions.


\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig2_ladder.pdf}
\caption{Oracle ladder ($n = 200$). Bars: semantic accuracy on judged rows,
Wilson 95\% CIs. Correction moves the final rung in opposite directions:
LoCoMo OM$\to$OC becomes significant (0.54 $\to$ 0.015); LongMemEval stops
being significant (0.041 $\to$ 0.078).}
\label{fig:ladder}
\end{figure*}

Three observations:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The correction moves conclusions in both directions.} On LoCoMo,
counting abstentions as wrong makes OM$\to$OC \emph{significant}
(0.541 $\to$ 0.0146); on LongMemEval it makes the same rung
\emph{non-significant} (0.041 $\to$ 0.078). Oracle modes absorb the most new
E0 rows (OM $+25$, OC $+16$).
\item \textbf{Memory's cost is benchmark-dependent.} On LoCoMo, real memory
loses two-thirds of the judged accuracy oracle memory recovers, and perfecting
context still adds a significant rung (the remaining loss is
retrieval/context); on LongMemEval the recovery is smaller and the last rung
does not separate. A single ``memory costs $X$'' number would be an artifact
of benchmark choice.
\item \textbf{The ladder is a control, not a ceiling.} OM/OC absorb E0 (43\% of
LoCoMo questions are gate-insufficient), so rungs are read with the
\S\ref{sec:audit} caveat, never as error rates.
\end{itemize}

\section{Failure-stage localisation is architecture-conditional}
\label{sec:localisation}

\subsection{Three systems with known bottlenecks}

We run three systems through the same questions, judge, gate, deterministic
floors, and 6000-character context budget: \emph{semantic} (LLM-extracted
atomic facts with provenance and supersession; designed bottleneck:
formation), \emph{summary} (one LLM summary per session; bottleneck: context),
and \emph{verbatim} (raw turns, cosine retrieval; bottleneck: retrieval). The
bottlenecks are named because the systems were authored with them: a stage
difference is evidence about architecture, not about memory agents in general
(\S\ref{sec:localisation:gate}).

\subsection{Same accuracy, different failure stage (LoCoMo $n = 100$)}

Figure~\ref{fig:fingerprint} and Appendix Table~\ref{tab:fingerprint} report
the one-batch run (100 questions $\times$ 3 systems, shared per-question gate,
errors 0; auto labels; accuracy = semantic accuracy on judged rows).


\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig3_fingerprint.pdf}
\caption{Failure-stage distributions, LoCoMo $n = 100$. Widths are shares of
100 questions; numerals are counts. End accuracy is indistinguishable, yet
stage distributions differ for all three pairs (statistics in text).}
\label{fig:fingerprint}
\end{figure*}

End accuracy is indistinguishable across all pairs (McNemar
$p = 0.736/0.728/0.864$; bootstrap CIs include 0), but the stage distributions
differ for \textbf{all three pairs}: Stuart--Maxwell
$p = 7.4\times10^{-5}$ (semantic vs summary), $2.7\times10^{-4}$ (semantic vs
verbatim), $5.0\times10^{-5}$ (summary vs verbatim); Holm-adjusted
$\leq 2.8\times10^{-4}$; the permutation test agrees. Per-label bootstrap CIs
isolate the pattern: semantic's F1 share $+0.18$ over both others
$[0.11, 0.26]$; summary's F5 share $+0.21$ over verbatim $[0.13, 0.29]$ and
$-0.20$ vs semantic $[-0.29,-0.12]$; verbatim's F4 share $+0.21$ over semantic
$[0.12,0.30]$ and $+0.25$ over summary $[0.15,0.35]$.

\subsection{The distribution result does not depend on E0 or on the automatic gate}

Two sensitivity results, both pre-registered as Stage A of the decision plan:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{E0-excluded.} Dropping the 42 gate-insufficient questions (58
used), all three pairs remain significant ($p = 2.0/7.5/2.7\times10^{-5}$;
Holm $\leq 7.6\times10^{-5}$): the headline does not depend on the
(upward-biased) E0 class.
\item \textbf{Label-noise sensitivity.} Monte Carlo over the stored
$n{=}100$ labels (300 draws per cell): each row's label is redrawn with
probability $q$ from (A) the empirical auto$\to$human confusion from the
40-row pilot, or (B) a uniform swap over observed classes. Under (A) all three
pairs keep Stuart--Maxwell $p < 0.05$ in \textbf{100\% of draws for all}
$q \leq 0.5$: the conclusion survives the misclassification the instrument
actually exhibits. Under (B), survival at $q = 0.5$ falls to 0.31/0.73/0.88
(semantic--verbatim, semantic--summary, summary--verbatim) but stays
$\geq 0.88$ at $q \leq 0.2$. The headline does not need per-row correctness,
but is not immune to arbitrary relabeling.
\item \textbf{Pilot consistency.} On the 40 annotated rows, human and automatic
labels agree on each system's dominant stage (semantic: formation; verbatim:
retrieval).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ablations and the pre-registered gate: partial, not passed}
\label{sec:localisation:gate}

Stage B of the frozen plan tests whether the fingerprint tracks architecture:
turn off a system's own bottleneck and check whether its dominant stage moves.
The pre-registered rule requires, for \textbf{at least two of three} systems,
(i) a dominant-share move of \textbf{$\geq 20$ points} and (ii) within-system
Stuart--Maxwell $p < 0.05$ after Holm. Two conditions ran (A2 was deferred:
a smoke test showed context assembly, not summary lossiness, binds); each is a
self-contained paired $n{=}100$ batch, never compared cross-batch.

\textbf{A1 --- remove the formation bottleneck} (semantic vs
\texttt{semantic\_rawwrite} write-through verbatim storage). The mechanism responds as pre-registered: formation recall
0.473 $\to$ 0.990, F1 13 $\to$ 0, and F4 doubles (12 $\to$ 24) as the loss
relocates downstream. Per-label bootstrap: F1 $+0.13$ $[0.07, 0.20]$, F4
$-0.12$ $[-0.22, -0.03]$. The within-system
Stuart--Maxwell is significant ($\chi^2 = 19.75$, df $= 6$,
\textbf{$p = 0.0031$}, Holm 0.0031; permutation $p = 0.0394$)---the first
ablation whose stage test clears $\alpha$ itself. Accuracy is unchanged
(0.500 vs 0.430, McNemar $p = 0.392$, CI $[-0.054, +0.185]$): same system,
same accuracy, different failure location. \textbf{But the largest share move
is 13 points---below the 20-point bar.}

\textbf{A3 --- remove the retrieval bottleneck} (verbatim $\to$ hybrid
ranking): recall 0.197 $\to$ 0.402, F4 share 0.37 $\to$ 0.23, accuracy
0.351 $\to$ 0.527 (McNemar $p = 0.014$), Stuart--Maxwell $p = 0.066$
(permutation 0.0087). \textbf{14 points, below the bar; the test does not
clear Holm.}

\textbf{Verdict: partial.} 0 of 2 evaluated conditions pass both criteria. Two
protocol facts travel with the verdict: with A2 deferred, only two of three
systems were evaluable, so the ``$\geq$2 of 3'' rule \textbf{could not} pass
as designed; and the bar couples to base rate---F1 was 13\% of rows, so
eliminating it moves the share by at most 13 points. Direction was met;
magnitude was not. Per the
frozen plan we \textbf{narrow the claim} to architecture-conditional
localisation and retract the universal fingerprint (N1); the bar is not
restated after the fact.

\subsection{Cross-benchmark check: qualitative only}

On the only available full-haystack LongMemEval sample ($n = 10$, ${\sim}$50
sessions per record), failure labels again differ across systems (verbatim: 4
retrieval losses; summary: retrieval/context; semantic: none). At $n = 10$
this is a sanity check, not evidence.

\subsection{Stage localisation is not shown to be actionable (N2)}

The pre-registered intervention widened hybrid candidates on the 35 F4
questions. The mechanism worked (recall 0.043 $\to$ 0.321) but the outcome
missed the rule ($\geq 6$ net flips \textbf{and} CI lower bound $> 0$):
\textbf{+7} net flips, McNemar $p = 0.143$, CI $[-0.029, +0.441]$. A no-op
placebo re-ask flipped 9/35 (${\approx}26\%$)---the net sits in retest noise.
A stage-agnostic composite reaches 0.563 overall (vs 0.429) but is
\emph{worse} on the 65 non-F4 questions (42 $\to$ 38), so the repair is not
stage-exclusive. \textbf{Measurement and intervention validity differ}: the F4
class is real and recall moved as designed, but the intervention is not shown
to improve answers; the null concerns actionability, not the attribution
signal.

\section*{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single-benchmark headline.} C4 rests on LoCoMo $n{=}100$; the
second real-haystack benchmark is an $n{=}10$ pilot. External validity is
open.
\item \textbf{Embedding backend.} All runs use the deterministic
\texttt{hash:384} fallback; comparisons hold only within it. The retrieval
bottleneck is not an embedding artifact: with real bge-m3 embeddings, F4 dense
recall moves only 0.043 $\to$ 0.100 (hybrid 0.321 $\to$ 0.343) and overall
dense recall falls (0.106 vs 0.205).
\item \textbf{Single judge.} No second-model protocol was approved;
self-consistency is perfect ($\kappa = 1.0$) but cross-family robustness is
unmeasured.
\item \textbf{Instrument caveats travel with the numbers.} Fingerprint labels
use the automatic gate (E0 biased upward); failure labels are 90\%
human-confirmed; the abstention correction is a validated rule; the consensus
gate covers 200 of 400 questions.
\item \textbf{Stage B evaluated two of three conditions}; the gate's pass rule
could not return a pass as designed
(\S\ref{sec:localisation:gate}).
\item \textbf{No head-to-head comparison.} The artifacts share no question set
with per-system traces and gold evidence, so \S\ref{sec:related} is
architectural, not an empirical ranking.
\item \textbf{Evolution (F3)} is measured only on a controlled probe; nothing
here quantifies evolution-stage loss in the wild.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Ethical considerations}

The study uses two public benchmarks and internal annotations; no new
human-subject data was collected. Disagreement data is reported in aggregate,
and the annotation packages are released so the corrections in
\S\ref{sec:audit} can themselves be re-audited.

\section{Protocol details and annotation failure modes}
\label{app:protocol}

\textbf{Label assignment order.} (1) semantically correct $\to$ no failure;
(2) no semantic verdict $\to$ \texttt{EVAL\_SEMANTIC\_UNCERTAIN}; (3) evidence
gate insufficient $\to$ \texttt{E0\_EVIDENCE\_INSUFFICIENT}; (4) gate not
sufficient or low confidence $\to$ \texttt{EVAL}; (5) Oracle-Context mode
$\to$ \texttt{RC\_REASONING\_CONTROL\_FAILURE}; (6) no gold evidence ids $\to$
\texttt{E0}; (7) first measured stage missing gold $\to$ F1/F2/F4/F5; (8) all
evidence present in context $\to$ \texttt{F6\_REASONING\_CANDIDATE}.

\textbf{Matching and provenance.} A gold item is matched by exact normalized
text or by token-overlap similarity $\geq 0.55$ among provenance-compatible
artifacts. Provenance is a constraint, not proof: an artifact whose turn
conflicts with the gold provenance cannot satisfy the item, and a match in an
artifact without provenance is reported separately rather than hidden.

\textbf{Round-2 gate pathology.} One annotator's ``missing fact'' notes were a
mechanical template lifting tokens from the reference answer: 75/81 quoted
tokens occur in the reference and 0/81 in the evidence, including function
words (``she'', ``because''). This is why the round-2 task shape could not
calibrate the gate and round 3 moved to per-fact binary judgements.

\section{Reproducibility details}
\label{app:repro}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Frozen configuration.} Commit \texttt{b8b1f6e}; freeze
\texttt{docs/PROTOCOL\_FREEZE.md}; post-freeze changes are logged in
\texttt{docs/PRE\_REGISTRATION\_CHANGELOG.md} before the affected run.
\item \textbf{Models and statistics.} DeepSeek-v4.1-flash produced every
answer, judgement, and gate decision (temperature 0); the embedding backend is
recorded per run. Tests: exact McNemar \citep{mcnemar1947} on discordant
pairs; paired bootstrap (5--10k repeats, seed 42); Stuart--Maxwell
\citep{stuart1955,maxwell1970} with Holm correction \citep{holm1979};
max-abs paired permutation (10k, seed 42); Wilson CIs \citep{wilson1927};
Cohen's $\kappa$ \citep{cohen1960}.
\item \textbf{Code and data.} All protocol, tracer, evaluator, runner, and
analysis code is in the repository (232 unit tests pass); every number
regenerates from stored rows by label-only recomputation via the scripts in
Appendix~\ref{app:map}. LoCoMo and LongMemEval are public; annotation packages
and \texttt{results/analysis\_*.json} are released, and raw rows are
reconstructable from recorded run metadata. All runs are CPU-only apart from
the answer/judge/gate API.
\end{itemize}
